Before you start

What you're agreeing to.

This interview asks about how you use AI at work. Your answers help your team build a shared agreement. Before you begin, here's how your responses are handled.

Aggregated, never individual.

Your lead sees patterns by role — never your individual answers. Nothing shows on the dashboard until at least three people in your group have responded.

In a team this small, anonymity isn't absolute. Groups under three get merged with a related one. That's why seven people is the minimum.

You're not identified — at all.

No name, no email, no account. You arrive through a shared link and leave no trace tied to you.

The trade-off: once you submit, answers can't be edited or withdrawn — there's no record of “you” to find. Changed your mind partway? Just close the tab.

What's collected.

Six areas: which tools you use, how often, what data goes in, how you review output, what guidance you've had, and where the gaps are. Mostly multiple-choice, with a few optional free-text fields.

How long it's kept.

Aggregated results are kept so future rounds can track what changes. Individual answers are never stored on their own.

What you can do.

Stop anytime — partial answers are discarded. When you finish, you'll see a private summary, shown only in your browser. Change any answer with the back button.

Team details.

A note on minimum size

This process works best for teams of seven or more. For smaller teams, a facilitated conversation is more effective than a structured interview, because the aggregation needed to protect anonymity breaks down with fewer people.

Setup · Step 02 / 02

Define the roles in your team.

The interview adapts its questions based on what each person does. A creative designer and a media buyer use AI in different ways and handle different kinds of data, so they'll see different follow-up questions.

Roles

Group your team by function rather than individual job title. Each group needs at least three people so that individual responses can't be identified in the dashboard. For example, rather than listing Writer, Editor, and Copywriter separately, group them as "Content team, 5 people." The grouping should reflect how people's work overlaps — roles that handle similar data and tools belong together.

Why three is the minimum

Each team member selects their group before starting the interview, and the dashboard shows results by group. With fewer than three people in a group, the lead could identify individual responses even in aggregate. The tool requires every group to have at least three people before interviews can launch.

About · How this tool was built

Six principles, one prototype.

The principles below shaped the tool. They come out of a now well-documented pattern: AI use in workplaces is widespread, fragmented, and largely unmanaged. 81% of employees use unapproved AI tools at work,[1] 56% lack clear guidance,[2] and only 36% of companies have formal AI governance in place.[2] AI use has grown faster than the shared understanding of how to use it.

01

Find out before you write.

The interview comes first. Policy built on what the team actually does beats policy written in the abstract.

02

Anonymity has limits — and the tool says so.

Quotes get edited, transcripts stay hidden from the lead, and seven people is the floor. Where anonymity can't be guaranteed, it's named out loud.

03

See the picture before deciding.

The dashboard opens before the workshop. You absorb what's actually happening, then make the calls.

04

The workshop has opinions.

Each decision area opens with a recommendation, grounded in research and your team's data — a position to argue with, not a default to accept.

05

The lead takes the interview too.

Same questions, same aggregation, same dashboard. The lead is one voice in the team, not above it.

06

The agreement is meant to be revisited.

Every ninety days the team re-interviews and the agreement updates. AI moves faster than annual policy cycles.

References [1] UpGuard, The State of Shadow AI, 2025. upguard.com
[2] SQ Magazine, Shadow AI Usage Statistics 2026, summarizing Microsoft, LinkedIn, Pew, and BlackFog/Sapio Research surveys, 2025–2026. sqmagazine.co.uk

Who built this

Elzanne Amao

A content strategist based in Cape Town, sixteen years across education marketing, technology, and communications. Most of her recent work focuses on how small teams use AI in practice rather than in theory.

She built this tool because the questions it asks are the questions she's been working through inside her own work. The gap between what teams do with AI and what they think they're doing turned out to be wider than she expected, and the existing tools for closing that gap mostly didn't fit the small teams she knew best.

Her writing on AI and the humans inside the systems we're building lives at elzanneamao.substack.com. She holds a Master's in Strategic Communication from the University of Johannesburg.

This tool is a prototype. The methodology behind it is being used at her own workplace as part of an AI policy implementation, and what's learned from that work will shape future versions.
